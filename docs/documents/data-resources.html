The DUO Data: Background & Codebook

This module uses public data from DUO (Dienst Uitvoering Onderwijs), the Dutch government agency that administers and publishes education data. The data is about the doorstroomtoets, the test every Dutch student takes at the end of primary school, and the debate about whether that test is fair. This page gives you the background you need to understand the data, a codebook for every file, and a list of pitfalls to watch out for.

You don’t need to be Dutch, or know anything about the Dutch education system, to work with this data. You do need the handful of terms below, because they show up directly as column names and category labels.

Getting the data

The data is downloaded by an R script, so nobody has to download files by hand and everything is reproducible:

  • In the worksheets, use data/get-data.R from the course repository (see the setup section of Worksheet 1).
  • For Assignment 2, use scripts/01-get-data.R in the starter project. It downloads the same four files and loads them into your R session.

Both scripts save the files in data/raw/. Never edit anything in that folder by hand, and never push data to GitHub. Anyone can recreate it by rerunning the script.

Background

The Dutch school system, just enough of it

Dutch primary school (basisonderwijs, PO) runs from groep 1 to groep 8, roughly ages 4 to 12. Most students attend a regular primary school (Bo); students who need extra support may attend a special primary school (Sbo, speciaal basisonderwijs).

At the end of groep 8, every student gets a school advice (schooladvies): the secondary-school (voortgezet onderwijs, VO) track that the student should go to. From the most practical to the most academic:

Track Full name What it is
PRO praktijkonderwijs practical education, for students who need the most support
VMBO-B basisberoepsgerichte leerweg preparatory vocational education, basic track
VMBO-K kaderberoepsgerichte leerweg preparatory vocational education, “framework” track
VMBO-GT gemengde/theoretische leerweg preparatory vocational education, mixed/theoretical track
HAVO hoger algemeen voortgezet onderwijs senior general secondary education
VWO voorbereidend wetenschappelijk onderwijs pre-university education

There is also VSO (voortgezet speciaal onderwijs), special-needs secondary education, which sits outside this ranking. A student can also get a combined advice, like VMBO-GT/HAVO, when the school thinks either track could fit.

The track a student starts in matters a lot: switching tracks later is possible, but not easy. This is why the advice, and everything that feeds into it, is a sensitive topic.

What is a doorstroomtoets?

Near the end of groep 8 (around February), every student takes a doorstroomtoets (“transfer test”; doorstroom = “flow-through”, toets = “test”). It is a standardized test of reading, language and maths. Its main purpose is to be an objective second opinion next to the teacher’s judgement: if a student scores higher than their teacher’s advice suggests, the school must reconsider (heroverweging) the advice. In principle, the advice can only be adjusted upwards.

The doorstroomtoets replaced the old eindtoets (“final test”, including the well-known Citotoets) from the 2023–2024 school year. Since then, schools can choose which doorstroomtoets their students take, from six approved providers:

Code in the data Test Publisher Notes
LIB Leerling in Beeld Cito B.V. successor of the old Citotoets; used by almost half of all schools
IEP IEP Eindtoets Bureau ICE used by about 40% of schools
ROUTE8 Route 8 A-VISION digital and adaptive
DIA Dia-Eindtoets Diataal BV
AMN AMN Eindtoets AMN B.V.
DOE Overheidsdoorstroomtoets Stichting Cito the free, government-funded test; used by less than 1% of schools

Each provider reports its scores on its own scale, so an IEP score of 80 and a LIB score of 180 can’t be compared. To make the tests comparable, all results are also translated into reference levels (referentieniveaus): standardized skill levels that are meant to mean the same thing, whichever test a student took.

  • 1F (fundamenteel niveau): the basic level, which nearly every student should reach.
  • 1S (maths) and 2F (reading and language): the higher target level (streefniveau).

The fairness debate

The idea behind letting schools choose was that all six tests are equivalent. A series of investigations by the newspaper de Volkskrant, starting in January 2025, found that they are not:

  • Reference levels differ by provider. For example, about 49% of students who took Route 8 reached reading level 2F, compared to about 80% of students who took the paper version of LIB. In maths, students who took LIB reached level 1S about twice as often as students who took Route 8.
  • School advice differs by provider, even more starkly. students who took AMN got a PRO or VMBO-B advice about 18 times as often as students who took paper LIB (14.6% vs. 0.8%). Paper LIB students got a VWO advice 15.4% of the time, AMN students 4.4%.
  • This is not (only) because different kinds of schools choose different tests. The obvious objection is that schools don’t choose a provider at random. Maybe schools with more disadvantaged students happen to prefer certain tests. The Volkskrant therefore compared schools with an equal schoolweging (a measure of how disadvantaged a school’s student population is, see the codebook). The gap between providers remained. In other words, it’s partly a property of the test itself.
  • Test format matters too. According to the College voor Toetsen en Examens (CvTE), which oversees the tests, 77% of students with a VWO teacher advice got a VWO test result on the paper LIB test, compared to 43% on the digital IEP test.

Critics called the doorstroomtoets an “ongelijkheidsmachine” (“inequality machine”): a test that was introduced to make educational opportunities more equal might be doing the opposite. Reporting on the 2026 results found that the gaps between providers had narrowed somewhat, but not disappeared. Schools with a more disadvantaged population also adjust their advice more often than other schools.

Where things stand. Sector organisations (PO-Raad, VO-raad) and the Dutch parliament pushed for a single national test. On 2 July 2026, the State Secretary for Education announced a plan to go from six providers back to one national doorstroomtoets by school year 2029/2030, stating that “full comparability is a utopia”: every difference in test design shifts the results.

Note

The numbers above come from the Volkskrant, CvTE and other sources (see Sources), which had access to student-level data. The DUO files you work with are school-level: you know how many students at a school reached each level, and which provider the school used, but not the results of individual students. Keep this in mind when you draw conclusions.

Codebook

All files contain data from the 2024–2025 school year, with one row per school location. The three DUO files (eindscores, referentieniveaus, schooladviezen) share the same identifying columns; schoolweging comes from a different organisation and is structured differently.

"<5" means “fewer than 5”

To protect students’ privacy, DUO hides every count below 5 and writes the text "<5" instead. This means that count columns are read into R as text, not numbers, and as.numeric() silently turns every "<5" into NA. See Pitfalls for how to deal with this.

Tips

Look out for these features in the data:

  1. One row is one school, not one student. Every number is a count or average for a whole school. A school with 10 students in groep 8 counts as one row, just like a school with 100. Think about whether you want to weigh schools by their number of students, and be careful with conclusions about individual students.

  2. Hidden counts ("<5"). Convert these explicitly instead of letting as.numeric() turn them into NA. In eindscores this affects only a few percent of schools. In referentieniveaus and schooladviezen it affects the large majority of schools, because students are split over many small categories, so dropping those schools is not an option. A reasonable approach is to replace "<5" with 2.5 (the midpoint of 1–4) and check whether your conclusion changes when you use 1 or 4 instead.

  3. Wide format. eindscores has a pair of columns per provider, instead of one “provider” column. Use pivot_longer() to get one row per school per provider, and keep the provider with the most students (about 96% of schools use exactly one provider). See Worksheet 1 for a worked example.

  4. Different score scales. Only compare providers on reference levels or school advice, never on the raw _GEM scores.
